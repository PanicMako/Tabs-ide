Tabs Exchange Privacy Policy

Version: 2026-10-20
Effective date: 20 October 2026
Operator: PanicMako (also known as Rushil), an individual running Tabs Exchange as a personal hobby project
Privacy and support contact: tabsappinbox@gmail.com

This notice concerns the Tabs Exchange website, publisher portal, registry API, and review/reporting service at https://exchange.buildwithtabs.com. It does not serve as a complete privacy policy for every feature of the Tabs desktop app or for independently operated third-party extensions. An extension publisher must explain its own practices where relevant.

This service is intended for people worldwide. This notice applies to the Exchange activities described here regardless of a user's country. Applicable privacy rights and our obligations can differ by location, the operator's establishment, and how services are offered; free access does not by itself remove those obligations.

1. Information involved in using Exchange

Browsing and downloads. Hosting and network providers receive ordinary web request information such as an IP address, request path, browser/client details, time, and response information. Search and package requests reach the registry. Providers manage their technical logs under their own retention and security practices. The Exchange frontend does not include advertising trackers or a third-party behavioural-analytics SDK.

Signing in. GitHub handles authentication. The Exchange stores your GitHub numeric account ID and login name. It uses the GitHub authorisation exchange to read that identity; the current registry code does not store the GitHub OAuth access token as a persistent account credential. We do not receive your GitHub password. GitHub has its own privacy practices.

Security and account records. We store sign-in session and anti-forgery token hashes, session expiry information, publisher-token metadata/hashes, namespace membership, invitations, roles, and agreement-version acceptance records. We also record reviewer-access and publisher-verification decisions and their supporting reasons/proof links.

Submitting an extension. We receive your package, manifest, version, description, README, images, licence/disclosure information, support/source/privacy links, scan evidence, and submission/review/appeal history. Package contents may include personal data if you put it there; avoid uploading unnecessary personal information or secrets.

Reports and communications. A private report includes your signed-in GitHub identity, the extension/version, reason, details, timestamps, and status. Authorised reviewers can add private investigation/decision notes. Information you send in a support, privacy, or legal request is also processed to handle that request.

2. Why the information is used

We use information to authenticate users; create and manage publisher identities; record agreement acceptance; operate submission, scanning, review and signed publication; deliver packages and compatibility information; investigate security, identity and policy concerns; administer access; maintain service integrity and backups; answer requests; and meet applicable legal obligations.

We do not use submitted private packages or private reports for unrelated advertising or AI-model training. Copying the developer page's agent instructions does not send your idea to a coding-agent provider through the Exchange. If you paste those instructions, code, or reports into another service, that disclosure is your separate action under that service's terms.

Where the law requires a legal basis for processing, we use information necessary to provide the account and publishing service you request, pursue legitimate interests in securing and administering the service where permitted, and meet applicable legal obligations. Where consent is required for an additional use, we will obtain it and provide a way to withdraw it. Withdrawing consent does not affect processing already lawfully carried out or processing on another applicable basis. We do not rely on one blanket consent for every use.

3. What becomes public and what stays restricted

Approved, signed releases and their listing materials, package identity, versions, digests and relevant verification information are public on a public registry. Downloaders can retain copies, and public content can be copied or indexed by others. Removing a listing cannot reliably recall those copies.

Quarantined submissions, account sessions/tokens, private reports, reviewer notes, and internal decision evidence are not public catalog information. The service restricts them to the relevant authorised account/team members, reviewers, operators, and necessary infrastructure providers. Review history visible to a publisher can differ from confidential internal evidence.

We may disclose the minimum information reasonably needed to investigate a concern or explain a decision to an affected publisher. Reports are not automatically forwarded to publishers. Do not promise absolute reporter anonymity: authorised staff and legally required recipients may need identifying information.

4. Service providers and other disclosures

The currently documented hosting arrangement uses Render for the registry service, Neon for PostgreSQL data, Cloudflare R2 for object storage, and GitHub for sign-in and source/release integration. These providers process the data necessary to perform their services and may process technical/security data under their own terms. A provider name is not a promise that data remains in one country. These providers publish their own privacy information: Render, Neon, Cloudflare and GitHub.

Email sent to tabsappinbox@gmail.com is handled using Google Gmail and its privacy policy. Please do not send secrets or unnecessary sensitive information by email.

We may also disclose information where required by a valid legal process, to protect people or service security, or to establish or defend legal rights. Where permitted, requests should be assessed for validity and disclosures limited to what is necessary. A business transfer affecting personal data would require appropriate safeguards and notice where the law requires it.

The Exchange does not sell personal information or run an advertising data brokerage in the reviewed implementation. External publishers and infrastructure providers have their own practices.

5. Cookies and local storage

The website uses an OAuth-state cookie during sign-in, an HttpOnly session cookie, and a readable anti-forgery cookie for authenticated requests. Current configured validity is approximately ten minutes for an OAuth attempt and seven days for a website session. Signing out clears the session cookies and invalidates that session. Infrastructure providers may also use cookies for security or delivery.

CLI publishing tokens currently expire after thirty days and can be revoked; that expiry is not a promise that their metadata or associated audit history is immediately deleted. The Exchange stores token hashes rather than the reusable secret value in its database.

The portal may store non-secret interface preferences in your browser. You can clear browser storage or block cookies, but sign-in and publisher actions may then stop working. Any future non-essential tracking will require updated disclosures and any required consent before it is introduced.

6. Retention and deletion

Different records serve different purposes. Account and membership records are needed while the account/publisher relationship remains active. Public release artifacts, security evidence, agreement acceptance and decision history may need to remain for integrity, investigation, disputes, licence duties, or legal requirements after an account stops publishing. Reports should be retained only as long as needed for their investigation and justified follow-up.

Expiry of a login or token disables access; it does not necessarily erase the database row. Backups and provider logs have their own lifecycle. There is no universal automatic deletion timer for registry records. We assess retention against the purposes above and applicable requirements, including when handling a closure or deletion request. You may contact us for information about a particular record.

You can request account closure or deletion through the privacy contact. We may verify identity and explain any information that must be retained. We assess requests record by record. We cannot promise immediate deletion of signed history, every backup, or copies already distributed to others. Where retention is necessary, we will explain the reason to the extent legally permitted.

7. Your choices and requests

Use the privacy contact above to ask what personal information we hold, request correction or deletion, raise a grievance, or withdraw a consent where applicable. Depending on the law in force and your location, you may have additional rights such as access to information, a copy, objection/restriction, nomination, or a complaint to a competent authority. We will assess the applicable rights and any exceptions rather than require you to waive them.

Please identify the account and request without sending passwords, authentication tokens, or unnecessary identity documents. We may request proportionate evidence to prevent someone else changing or deleting your data. We will respond without undue delay and within any applicable legal deadline. If you disagree with our response, reply to the same contact to request a further review or contact the competent privacy authority. We may need additional information or an extension where the law permits it and will explain this.

8. Security and international processing

The service uses HTTPS, hashed session/publishing credentials, access controls, CSRF protection, bounded package handling, and restricted reviewer interfaces. These measures reduce risk; they cannot guarantee perfect security. If a breach occurs, we will investigate and make notifications required by the laws that apply. The required notification process depends on the applicable law and incident circumstances.

Infrastructure or authorised personnel may process data in countries other than your own or the operator's country. International processing remains subject to applicable transfer requirements. Contact us for information about the providers and arrangements relevant to your data, including any applicable contractual safeguards.

9. Children and changes

Publisher accounts are intended for people legally able to enter the publisher agreement. Exchange account and publishing services are not directed at children. We do not offer a dedicated child-account or parental-consent flow. If you believe a child has provided personal information without the authorisation required by applicable law, contact us so we can assess and address it.

We will show a dated notice when this policy changes and provide additional notice or choices where required. A materially new processing purpose should not be hidden in an unrelated extension update. Contact tabsappinbox@gmail.com with questions about this notice.

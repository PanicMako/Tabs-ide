# X (Twitter) post kit for Tabs

Every post below fits in 280 characters (counted), so it works on a free account.

## How X is different from Reddit

- **Reddit wants the story in text. X wants the thing working, fast.** Most people scroll past without reading, so the video does the talking and the text stays short and casual.
- **Upload the video natively** (not a YouTube link). Native video gets the most reach. Keep it 30 to 60 seconds and show Tabs actually working within the first ~5 seconds: cluttered Mac, then switching between two project tabs.
- **No links in the main post.** Several 2026 guides report that X cuts reach for posts with outside links, sharply for non-Premium accounts. Put the download and GitHub links in the first reply.
- **Hashtags:** none, or one at most. More than that looks spammy and doesn't help.
- **Tone:** plain and lowercase is normal on dev Twitter. No "excited to announce", no "game-changer", no emoji walls. Describe what it does, without hype.
- **Replies matter more than likes.** Stay around for the first hour and answer everyone.

## Your account is about a month old

Be realistic: a launch thread from a new account with few followers will reach few people, and that's normal. X mostly shows posts to people who already engage with you. The fix is a week or two of replying before (and after) you post:

- Pick 10 to 20 accounts you actually read (indie devs, people building with Claude Code or Codex, front-end folks, Theo) and turn on notifications.
- Leave 5 to 10 **real** replies a day: answer the question, add an example, share what happened when you tried it. Skip "great point".
- Post small things as you go: a 10-second clip of a feature, a bug you fixed, something you learned. Then the launch thread isn't the first thing on your profile.
- Post the launch thread anyway. Even with low reach, it's the link you'll send people from LinkedIn, Reddit and job applications.

## About tagging

- **Theo (@theo):** tag him in the credits reply (reply 2), not the main post. In the reply, the tag reads as giving credit. In the main post, it can look like you're asking for a retweet. He has publicly said he likes seeing elaborate T3 Code forks, but don't ask him to share it.
- **Claude / Anthropic / OpenAI accounts:** I'd skip these. Big company accounts rarely respond, and tagging them in a first post can come across as fishing for attention.

## Step by step

1. **Get the video ready.** 30 to 60 seconds, as an MP4 file. In the first 5 seconds, click between two project tabs so people see the switch immediately.
2. **Pick the time.** Tue, Wed or Thu, 7:30 to 9:30 pm IST (US morning).
3. **Write the main post.** Paste it in and attach the video file. No links, no hashtags. Post it.
4. **Reply to your own post** straight away with reply 1. Then reply to that reply with reply 2, then 3, 4 and 5, so it all reads as one thread.
5. **Pin it.** On the main post, tap ••• > Pin to your profile.
6. **Stay for an hour.** Answer every reply, even short ones. Like and reply to quote posts.
7. **Share it where you already have people:** LinkedIn, friends, college groups. "Hey, I made this, would love your thoughts" is fine. Don't ask anyone for likes or retweets.
8. **Over the next week:** reply to the thread whenever you fix something ("fixed the bug someone found, v2.3.3 is out"). Keep doing your daily real replies on other people's posts.

## If you get pushback

- **"Another T3 Code fork."** "yep, said so in the thread. the part i added is the project tabs and the editor, browser and terminal living per project. totally fair if t3 code alone works for you"
- **"Why not just VS Code?"** "if one window works for you that's great. this is for jumping between a few projects without losing the browser and terminal that go with each"
- **Rude comment:** ignore it, or reply once politely. Don't quote-tweet to argue.
